# Gildroot Press: market research

Researched and written on 2026-09-30 for the Gildroot Press line (print books on Amazon KDP, and printable PDFs on gildroot.com). Every price, rating and review count below was read on 2026-09-30 unless a different date is given. Source numbers in square brackets refer to the list at the end.

## How this was researched, and what it could not reach

- **Amazon itself was not readable from here.** Product pages, review pages and bestseller lists returned a robot check or HTTP 503 on amazon.com, amazon.ca and amazon.co.uk. We did not try to get around the check. Amazon data therefore comes from three second-hand routes, and each quote in this file is labeled with the route it came from:
  - **Direct**: read on the page cited (Goodreads, a publisher's own store, Trustpilot, KDP help pages).
  - **Snapshot**: a third-party copy of an Amazon listing (selfpublishingtitans.com), which shows price, pages, trim, rating count and a few review excerpts. The copy is not dated, so treat counts as "recent", not "today".
  - **Summary**: a search engine's summary of an Amazon listing. The facts are usually right, but the wording of a review may be paraphrased. These are marked and are never used as the only evidence for a decision.
- **Reddit was not reachable** (HTTP 403, and search engines returned no r/Genealogy threads). The FamilySearch Community forum stood in for it.
- **Keyword volumes were not pulled.** The OpenSEO account returned `INSUFFICIENT_CREDITS`. Seasonality below is built from gift dates and event calendars instead of search data. This is open issue 1.

## What held true across all four categories

These six findings recur in every category. Each recommended title is designed around them.

1. **There is not enough room to write.** This is the most common specific complaint in both record books and journals. Examples: "The spaces for writing in the charts and logs are tiny" [G3]; no room "to even include born and passing dates for ancestors, let alone the city" (Summary) [S7].
2. **The type is too small or too faint for the person who has to fill it in.** "My grandma has a hard time reading it because the writing is small" (Snapshot) [P2]; "the print is very light and very small" (Snapshot) [P4]. EasyGenie sells "LARGE PRINT" genealogy forms "without the need for a magnifying glass" [E1]. Some competitors already sell large-print editions (Dragonfly Books' *Heirloom*, 2025 [S13]), so we cannot claim to be the only one. We can be exact about it: we will print the point size and the line spacing on the back cover.
3. **Books run out of pages.** "THE BOOK IS LIMITED IN HOW MANY COPIES THEY GIVE YOU ON EACH SUBJECT" [G1]; "they purposely did not include enough pedigree sheets" [G3]. Journal buyers hit the same problem from the other side: several grandchildren each want a filled copy (Snapshot) [P3].
4. **Families that are not a two-parent first marriage get left out.** "It's geared towards those with two parents, family vacations and unfortunately, that's not me" [Q1]. On Storyworth: "I had a horrible, abusive family and lived in poverty. None of these questions apply to the life I lived." [T1]. Where a competitor did include blended-family forms, reviewers noticed: "The worksheets are very inclusive" [G3].
5. **Cheap low-content clones lose trust.** KDP organizers draw reviews such as "only about 10 pages of charts but many blank notebook pages", "really cheaply made", a form that printed "Date of Birth" where "Date of Death" should be, "qustion" misspelled across a fifth of the book, and "no instructions" (all Summary) [S9, S10]. A 2026 wave of fast-published genealogy titles makes the shelf more crowded [S11].
6. **People praise lay-flat binding, and KDP cannot make it.** "I love work books that i can lay flat" [L1]. Peter Pauper uses concealed wire-o and Duncan & Stone uses a lay-flat spine [S7, D1]. KDP offers perfect-bound paperback and case-laminate hardcover only [K4]. What we can do: use a wide gutter so writing near the spine is comfortable, and sell the letter-size PDF for anyone who wants loose pages in a binder.

### What buyers praise

- Room and structure for a fixed number of ancestors. Heritage Hunter's "255 individuals" and "up to 16 pieces of information" are the headline of its listing, and *Discover Your Ancestors* magazine called it "the best-organised, most flexible family tree workbook we've seen" [HH1].

### Gaps we can fill

- **Room to write, specified in numbers.** Every place line runs the full width of the column, and every date field fits "24 May 1819" in handwriting of about 4 mm cap height (at least 9 mm between lines).
- **Complete numbered coverage.** All 255 ancestors of 8 generations, numbered with the Ahnentafel system, so person 38 is always the father of person 19. Every pedigree chart says where each line continues. Free continuation sheets for generations 9 and beyond are on gildroot.com, with no email asked for.
- **Charts drawn by the same engine as the wall charts.** A blank 5-generation fan and linked 4-generation pedigree charts come from `layout-fan.js` and `layout-pedigree.js` in the new blank mode. They are not clip-art trees. One filled sample spread uses Queen Victoria's ancestry from the CC0 Wikidata sample, checked by the fact-checker, so readers see a correct example without us inventing anyone.
- **Inclusive by structure, not by a sticker.** Each person page has "Partners" (plural) with the dates of each partnership, children listed under the partnership they came from, and a "Parents" block that records the kind of relationship for each parent: birth, adoptive, step, foster or guardian. Same-sex couples fill in "Partner / Partner" and nothing on the page assumes husband and wife. A section for "Other parents" is keyed to the Ahnentafel numbers, so an adoptive line and a birth line can both be recorded.
- **US-first record guidance** (census years 1790–1950, the lost 1890 census, when each state began birth and death registration), with a shorter page for England and Wales, Scotland, Ireland, Canada and Germany. The fact-checker must verify every date before print.
- **A proofreading and form-label gate** in `web/test/books/`, so "Date of Death" can never print as "Date of Birth".

### Seasonality

- Q4 gift season is the peak for family-keepsake books. For example, Bluestreak's *Grandmother's Journal* was one of Oprah's Favorite Things 2024 [BN2].
- October is Family History Month in the US [N1].
- Summer is reunion season, which suits the Organizer segment. We have no sales data for it yet.

### Gaps we can fill

- **Large print, stated exactly.** Prompts set at 14 pt in solid black (never grey), with answer lines 10 mm apart on a 7 × 10 page. The back cover will state these numbers.
- **Questions written for every kind of life.** Use "the people who raised you" rather than "your mother and father". Never assume marriage, children, religion or a comfortable childhood. Every chapter opens with a line such as "Skip any question. Answer the ones that fit your life." and offers two or three other ways into the same topic. The voice rules forbid jokes about adoption, illegitimacy or death, and the prompts follow the same rule.
- **One book for any parent or grandparent,** personalized by a dedication page, not a pile of near-identical gendered editions. Near-duplicates also risk KDP's rule against "minimally differentiated" books [K5].
- **Enough copies for the whole family.** The PDF edition lets families print extra blank copies. Pages are designed to photocopy and scan cleanly (no tints, page numbers on every page), so one filled book can be copied for each grandchild.
- **A tree page drawn by the engine.** A 4-generation fan with the storyteller at the center, plus a descendants page with room for step-grandchildren and partners. Grandmother Remembers' newer editions advertise a "frameable family tree", which suggests buyers value this page [S14].
- **No subscription, no typing, no app.** Everything is on paper at one price. We can state facts about the difference; we must not disparage Storyworth (voice rule 4).
- **Original prompts only.** Many questions are common across the category ("Where did you grow up?"). We must not copy any competitor's list or order. A check against the text of the best-selling books is open issue 5.

### Seasonality

Mother's Day (9 May 2027), Father's Day (20 June 2027), US Grandparents Day (the first Sunday after Labor Day: 12 September 2027) and Christmas. These are calendar dates; we have no search-volume curve (open issue 1).

### Gaps we can fill

- **Every form sized for handwriting,** at US Letter size so the book and the PDF are page-for-page the same. PDF buyers print only the forms they need, as often as they need them. This answers the "too few copies" complaint directly.
- **Census extraction sheets that use each census's real column headings.** US federal censuses 1850–1950 are public-domain government records. Add England and Wales 1841–1921. The fact-checker must check every column against the original schedule.
- **A research log that asks for a proper source citation** (who, what, when, where in the record), plus a "negative search" column. Serious researchers value recording what was searched and not found.
- **A DNA match tracker** with columns for shared cM, the longest segment, the testing company and a shared-match cluster, plus a clustering worksheet. A reference table of relationship ranges can be adapted from the Shared cM Project, which is licensed CC BY 4.0 with attribution to Blaine Bettinger [DNA1]. The attribution and a check of the version are required before print.
- **Charts from the engine:** an Ahnentafel index chart, and a relationship chart computed by the same logic as `web/src/app/tools/cousin.js`.
- **Real instructions, and only as many as help:** one short, worked example per form, not padding. The instruction pages also make the book a proper book with an ISBN rather than a low-content log [K5].

  - Prompts at 14 pt in solid black; answer lines 10 mm apart.
- **Contents:**
  - Seven chapters: early years, the people who raised you, school and work, love and partnership, home and family (with no assumption of children), the world you lived through, and what you want remembered.
  - Quick-answer pages.
  - An engine-drawn 4-generation fan with the storyteller at the center, and a descendants page.
  - Photo pages.
  - One Gildroot page at the back.
- **Review complaints it fixes:**
  - "the writing is small" [P2] and "very light and very small" [P4] → 14 pt solid black type on a 7 × 10 page.
  - "geared towards those with two parents, family vacations" [Q1] and "None of these questions apply to the life I lived" [T1] → open framing, permission to skip, and other ways into each topic.
  - "odd or personal" questions [P3] → sensitive topics are clearly optional and gently worded.
  - Several grandchildren wanting a copy [P3] → the PDF edition, and pages that photocopy cleanly.
  - Typing and apps [T1] → paper only; no subscription.
- **Why it ranks second:**
  - It is the biggest market by far: the top title has more than 12,000 ratings.
  - It is also the most crowded, with brands that have thousands of reviews.
  - We win only with a specific difference (large print, inclusive questions and a real tree page) and cannot outspend the leaders.
  - It sells well at every gift date, so it smooths the chart business's Q4 peak.

### 3. The Research Ledger: logs, census extractions, letters and DNA matches

- **Positioning line:** *Every form a working family historian uses, sized for handwriting, with the real column headings for every US census from 1850 to 1950.*
- **Format:**
  - 8.5 × 11 in paperback ($19.99), white paper.
  - 200 pages.
  - Also sold as the same pages in a printable PDF ($12, subject to owner approval).
  - No hardcover, because a desk tool needs to be cheap to replace.
- **Contents:**
  - How to use the forms, with a worked example of each: 12 pages.
  - Research plans.
  - Research logs with citation and "searched, not found" columns.
  - US census extraction sheets for 1850–1950, one per census year, and England and Wales 1841–1921.
  - Vital-record and probate abstracts.
  - A correspondence and records-request log.
  - DNA match tracker and a clustering worksheet.
  - Shared cM ranges table (CC BY 4.0, with attribution).
  - Ahnentafel index.
  - Relationship chart.
  - Brick-wall planner.
  - One Gildroot page at the back.
- **Review complaints it fixes:**
  - "spaces ... are tiny" [G3] → letter-size forms with handwriting-sized fields.
  - "did not include enough pedigree sheets" [G3] → the PDF edition, printable without limit.
  - "no instructions ... a blank pad of paper" and misspellings [S10] → a worked example on every form, and a spelling and label test.
  - Running out of child rows [E1] → family group sheets with 16 child rows.
- **Why it ranks third:**
  - It serves the Keeper, our primary segment, and the PDF edition may earn more than the paperback.
  - But free forms from NEHGS and FamilySearch set a price anchor of $0 [AA1], and the category is filling with fast-published titles [S11].

### 4. Two Families: a keepsake of the families joined by a marriage

- **Positioning line:** *Both families on one page: a bowtie chart with four generations on each side.*
- **Format:** printable PDF on gildroot.com first. If it sells, a hardcover at 8.25 × 11 in, 96 pages, $29.99 (royalty $10.50).
- **Review complaints it fixes:** none found, because no comparable book exists (see Category 4). Instead it answers the brief's Segment 3 job: "I want a picture of two families becoming one."
- **Why it ranks fourth:** there is no proven demand for it as a book. The wedding market buys decor and custom prints [J1, W2]. The idea is best tested cheaply on our own site.

## One Gildroot page per book (the funnel)

The last page of every book carries one small engine-drawn chart and the following two lines, and nothing else:

> When your tree lives in a family tree file, Gildroot can print it as a chart to frame. Your file is read on your computer. Nothing is uploaded.
> gildroot.com/press

- The page it points to lists the free continuation sheets and the chart products. It does not ask for an email address, which satisfies KDP's link rules [K6] and our privacy line.
- There are no ads anywhere else in the books, and no offers on the covers.

## Considered and not recommended now

- **A book of blank charts.** There is real demand: a FamilySearch Community user asked how to print a blank fan chart and learned that the FamilySearch fan chart only exists once data is entered [FS1]. TreeSeek and EasyGenie sell blank fan charts at 17 × 22 in [E2, S17]. But fan charts need poster sizes that KDP cannot print, and a book's gutter cuts through them. Better as a free download on gildroot.com drawn by the blank mode of the engine. It is a good lead magnet with no email gate.
- **A children's or school family-tree workbook with adoption-friendly layouts** (a "roots and branches" tree, a family wheel) [AF1]. This is a real need, but it has a different reader and a different design system. Revisit after title 2 if Grandparents Day sales show demand from grandparents doing it with grandchildren.
- **Separate gendered editions of the journal** ("for Grandma", "for Grandpa"). These are the category's search terms, but near-duplicate interiors risk KDP's "minimally differentiated" rule [K5]. Better: one interior and one title, with gendered terms only where they are honest (for example, in the description: "for a mother, father, grandmother or grandfather").

Then re-check the ranking, especially title 4.

3. **Amber: publishing under the owner's KDP account** and uploading the files (ops/README.md).
4. **Amber: buying ISBNs** ($295 for ten from Bowker) for a "Gildroot Press" imprint name. Without them, KDP's free ISBNs show "Independently published".
5. **An originality check for the journal prompts** against the best-selling journals, so we copy no one's list or order.
6. **KDP's AI disclosure.** Any AI-drafted text must be declared when publishing [K7]. The owner decides how much of the prompt text they rewrite, and declares honestly either way.
7. **Fact-checker sign-off** before print on every record date and census column heading, on the Victoria sample spread, and on the Shared cM table and its attribution.
8. **Owner approval for the PDF prices** ($12 each proposed).
9. **The lay-flat gap.** KDP cannot print spiral or lay-flat bindings. Lulu offers coil binding, but only for direct orders, not retail distribution [LU1]. Using it would be a new paid service (Amber); revisit only if reviews ask for it.
