Paper: matte or photo

Most charts are fine print on a light ground, and read best without glare.

Matte poster paper
No shine, so names read under a lamp and behind glass. The first choice for Ivory, Botanical, Letterpress, Nordic and Cartographer.
Photo paper
Luster, satin or glossy. Deeper darks and richer color, with some reflection. The Midnight Gilt style prints best on photo paper or matte poster paper, never on plain paper.
Plain office paper
Fine for a desk copy or a test print. For home tiles, a heavier paper than everyday copy paper shows less through from the back, if your printer takes it.

Before a large order, print one Letter sheet at home at 100% to check spellings and colors. Screens are brighter than paper; dark grounds come out darker in print.

Three ways to print it

The same day, at a store

Many drugstore photo counters and office print shops print posters within hours. Photo counters usually take a JPEG and print on photo paper; print shops usually take a PDF. Sizes, papers and pickup times vary by store, so call or check their website first. Gildroot exports both: a vector PDF at the exact size, and a JPEG at 200 dpi up to 18×24 and 150 dpi above that.

Online, delivered

Online printers offer more papers and can frame the print for you. They usually need several business days, plus shipping, and holiday cut-off dates vary by company. Order early for a gift.

At home, on Letter or A4 sheets

A tiled print splits the poster across ordinary sheets you trim and tape together. See the steps below.

When you send a chart to a printer, that file goes to them. Gildroot never sees it, and never sees your family tree file.

Printing a poster at home

A 24×36 fan takes 15 Letter sheets plus the map page. Allow an hour, a ruler, a craft knife or sharp scissors, and clear tape.

Our drawing of the assembly map for a 24×36 poster on Letter sheets: 3 columns, 5 rows, each sheet labeled by row and column.

  1. In the studio, choose the poster size you want, then export Tiled home print on Letter or A4.
  2. Print every page at 100%, or “Actual size.” Turn off “Fit to page,” or the pieces will not line up.
  3. Page 1 is the assembly map. Every other sheet is labeled, such as “Row B · Column 3.” Lay them out in order on a table.
  4. Trim the white margin from the left and top of each sheet, along the crop marks. Leave the first column’s left edge and the first row’s top edge untrimmed.
  5. Neighboring sheets share a quarter inch of the picture. Lay each trimmed edge over its neighbor so the ticks line up, then tape along the back.
  6. Trim the outer edge, then mount it on foam board or poster board before framing, so the joins stay flat.

Questions

Which size should I choose?

Count the generations you want, then pick the smallest size that holds them. For a fan: 6 generations fit on 11×14, 7 on 16×20 or 18×24, and 8 on 20×30 or 24×36. For a gift of 4 generations or fewer, 11×14 is plenty, and the keepsake layout uses the extra room for larger type.

I made my chart on an iPad. Is the picture sharp enough?

The PDF is vector, so it is sharp at any size, on any device. The JPEG for photo counters is limited on iPhone and iPad to 16.7 megapixels, so a 24×36 JPEG comes out at about 139 dpi, not 150. We label it honestly. Most stores print that well; for the sharpest result, send the PDF.

Does the printer need a bleed?

Some print shops ask for one. The PDF export can add a 0.125 inch bleed and crop marks. Every chart also keeps a margin inside its own edge, at least 6% of the short side, so no name sits near the trim.
