For a wedding

Two families, one chart.

A bowtie chart puts the couple in a medallion at the center, with one family fanning out to the left and the other to the right. It is a picture of two families joining.

Why a bowtie for a wedding

Most family trees are about one person. A wedding is about two people and the two families behind them, and the bowtie is the chart made for that.

  • Both names sit in the center, with an ampersand, the wedding date and the place.
  • Each family gets the same space, so neither side looks like an afterthought.
  • Guests find their own side from the family names in the top corners.
  • It works for every couple. The studio does not assume a husband and a wife.

Up to 5 generations a side on a 16×20 or 18×24 poster, and 6 on 24×36. See the bowtie chart page for every size.

Ways to use it

At the guest-book table
Framed at 18×24 on an easel. Guests find their grandparents and talk about them.
At the rehearsal dinner
A chance for the two families to see where the other one comes from.
A gift from a parent
Given the night before, with a dedication line under the title, such as “For Anna and Sam, with love.”
Later, as an anniversary gift
The same chart, re-exported for the first anniversary in a different style. Re-exports are unlimited.

When to start

The chart itself takes about a minute. Gathering names and printing take longer.

  1. 6 to 8 weeks before: collect names from both families. If you have no family tree file, type in what you know in the studio and send an Ask-the-family link. Relatives fill in blanks on their phones, and the answers travel back inside the link, not through us.
  2. 4 weeks before: make the chart and check every name and date with someone from each family. Spellings matter to people.
  3. 3 weeks before: order the print. Online poster printers usually need several business days plus shipping. Leave room for a reprint.
  4. 2 weeks before: frame it. Standard 16×20 and 18×24 frames are sold in most craft and home stores.
  5. Running late? Many drugstore photo counters and office print shops print posters the same day. Call ahead and ask which sizes and papers they have.
