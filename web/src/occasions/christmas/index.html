For Christmas

A gift with their family in it.

For the parent who has everything: their parents, grandparents and great-grandparents, typeset and framed, with a line of your own under the title.

Why a small chart makes a good gift

At 4 generations or fewer, Gildroot sets the chart as a keepsake: larger type, full dates and birthplaces, and an ornamental frame. Fifteen people, each one easy to read.

You do not need a family tree file. The studio’s Quick Builder takes up to 15 people: the person the gift is for, their parents, grandparents and great-grandparents. For each one, a name, a birth year (or “about”), and a birthplace.

Missing a few names? Send an Ask-the-family link to an aunt or cousin. They fill in the blanks on their phone and send it back, and the answers travel inside the link, not through us. Ask them to keep it a surprise.

When to print it

Christmas Day 2026 is a Friday. Printers get busy in December, so earlier is calmer.

  1. Early December: order online. Online poster printers usually need several business days to print, plus shipping, and holiday cut-off dates vary by company. Check the one you use.
  2. The week before: many drugstore photo counters and office print shops print posters the same day. Call ahead and ask about sizes and pickup times.
  3. The night before: print a Letter-size copy at home on good paper and frame it in a standard 8.5×11 frame, or in a larger frame with a mat.
  4. Too late to print? Give a key instead. The recipient makes the chart whenever they are ready, and you can print a gift card to go with it.

Choosing a style

Botanical
Warm white with a laurel at the base, shown above. Suits a living room.
Midnight Gilt
Gold on midnight blue. Looks festive, and needs photo paper or matte poster paper to print well.
Ivory
Sepia on bone, with a double rule. The safest on any paper.
