For a family reunion

Everyone on one wall.

A 24×36 fan chart on an easel near the food table. People find their own grandparents, point, and start telling stories.

Which chart for a reunion

A fan chart puts the closest generations in the largest type, at the center, which matters when a crowd is standing around it. Put someone from the youngest generation in the center, and every ancestor the family shares appears in the rings behind them.

Choose the Family line color mode and the father’s side and mother’s side print in two different tints. Or choose Atlas to color everyone by the country they were born in, which starts a lot of conversations.

If the reunion is for one branch, center the chart on a grandchild of that branch, so the shared ancestors fill the outer rings.

Size and printing

24×36 for a room
Holds a fan of 8 generations: 255 names. The inner rings read from a few steps away; the outer rings reward a closer look.
Several copies
Re-exports are unlimited, so print one for the wall and a Letter copy for each household to take home.
On a budget
Print the poster at home in pieces on ordinary Letter paper, trim and tape. The print planner shows how.
Charts for other families
Reunion organizers and societies who make charts for others use the Professional license: $149 once, with a “Prepared by” line.

A timeline for organizers

  1. 2 months before: find the family member who keeps the tree and ask for a GEDCOM file. Our export guides cover Ancestry, MyHeritage, FamilySearch and the desktop programs.
  2. 6 weeks before: send an Ask-the-family link to each branch so they can add names and birth years from their phones.
  3. 4 weeks before: make the chart, and send a picture of it round the family to catch misspellings.
  4. 3 weeks before: order the poster online, or book a local print shop. Leave time for a second print if something needs fixing.
  5. On the day: bring the cousin calculator on a phone. Someone will ask how they are related to someone else.
