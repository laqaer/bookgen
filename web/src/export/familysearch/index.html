Export guide

Get your tree out of FamilySearch.

FamilySearch Family Tree is one shared tree for everyone, and the website has no button to download your part of it. The usual route is a free desktop program that signs in to FamilySearch, copies your ancestors to your computer, and saves them as a GEDCOM file.

Steps checked against FamilySearch’s own help pages on 30 September 2026. Gildroot is independent and not affiliated with FamilySearch.

With RootsMagic Essentials (free)

RootsMagic runs on Windows and Mac. Every download runs as RootsMagic Essentials, the free version, until a license key is entered. Steps from RootsMagic 11’s help pages.

  1. Download RootsMagic from rootsmagic.com (the “Try RootsMagic” page) and install it. Name and email are optional.
  2. Open RootsMagic. On the File page, click Create a New RootsMagic file. (With no file open, the Home page also has New File.)
  3. Choose Download from an online tree, then choose FamilySearch.
  4. Pick a folder, type a name for the new file, choose the options, and click OK.
  5. Sign in to FamilySearch when asked. Your web browser opens the “Sign in to FamilySearch” page; after you sign in, RootsMagic is signed in too.
  6. Choose what to download: for a chart, the ancestors of the person the chart is about, and as many generations as the chart will show (a fan holds up to 8).
  7. When the download finishes, save it as GEDCOM: on the File page, choose Export Data, then GEDCOM File. Pick a folder, type a name, and click OK.

Or with Ancestral Quest Basics (free)

Ancestral Quest runs on Windows and Mac, and Ancestral Quest Basics is its free version. Its maker says most of the FamilySearch features are in Basics, including downloading ancestors.

  1. Download Ancestral Quest Basics from ancquest.com, install it, and start a new, empty family file.
  2. In the menu at the top, click FamilySearch, then Import Family Lines. Sign in to FamilySearch if asked.
  3. Choose Download Ancestors of FamilySearch Person and enter that person’s FamilySearch ID, from their person page on FamilySearch.
  4. Set how many generations to import, then click OK. A large download takes a while.
  5. Click File, then Export. Choose your options, click Export, name the file, and click Export again.

Why there is no download button

On Ancestry or MyHeritage, your tree is yours. FamilySearch Family Tree is one tree shared by everyone who uses it, so there is no private copy to hand you. What you download is a snapshot of your branch as it stands today; other people can change the shared tree afterwards.

FamilySearch briefly offered an eight-generation export on one of its own pages. As of 30 September 2026 that page says GEDCOM export is no longer supported there, and points to compatible family tree programs instead.

Also free: Gildroot fan charts go to 8 generations and 24×36, from any file. FamilySearch’s own fan chart is free too, and shows 4 to 7 generations of FamilySearch data (as of September 2026).

Stuck?

Still stuck after these? Write to support@gildroot.com and say which step. Please don’t attach your file; we never need it.

RootsMagic doesn’t show any FamilySearch options.

RootsMagic’s help says FamilySearch features must be switched on: open the Settings page, click General settings, and tick Enable FamilySearch Support.

The sign-in page closed before I finished.

RootsMagic shows a Reopen button while it waits for the sign-in. Click it to open the FamilySearch sign-in page again.

Do I have to buy the program?

No. RootsMagic Essentials and Ancestral Quest Basics are free. Both makers sell fuller versions; you don’t need them for this.

Can I do this on an iPad?

These programs run on Windows and Mac computers. Once you have the .ged file, you can open it in Gildroot on an iPad.
