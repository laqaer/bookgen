Export guide

Export your tree from Ancestry.

Six clicks on the Ancestry website give you a GEDCOM file: every person, date and place in your tree, as one plain-text file you keep. You need to be the tree’s owner. You don’t need a membership.

Steps checked against Ancestry’s own help pages on 30 September 2026. Gildroot is independent and not affiliated with Ancestry.

Steps, on a computer

Use the Ancestry website in a web browser on a computer. These are the steps from Ancestry’s help page, “Uploading and Downloading Trees.”

  1. Sign in at ancestry.com. Click the Trees tab and select your tree.
  2. In the toolbar on the left, click More (the three dots) and select Tree Settings.
  3. On the right side of the Tree Settings page, under Manage your tree, click Export tree. If you see Download your GEDCOM file instead, skip to step 5.
  4. Click Export. The button spins and says Generating a GEDCOM file. A large tree can take a few minutes.
  5. When it stops spinning, click Download Your GEDCOM File. It saves to your computer, usually in the Downloads folder.
  6. The GEDCOM file arrives inside a .zip file. Drop the .zip into Gildroot as it is, or unzip it first.

What comes out: every person, fact and relationship in the tree. Photos and other media stay on Ancestry; Ancestry says files made after November 2022 record where the media are saved, for re-uploading. See also printing your Ancestry tree.

Stuck?

Still stuck after these? Write to support@gildroot.com and say which step. Please don’t attach your file; we never need it.

I don’t see Export tree.

Only the owner of a tree can export it. If a relative owns the tree and shared it with you, ask them to follow these steps and send you the file.

It says “Generating” for a long time.

Ancestry’s help page says: if it has been generating for more than ten minutes, refresh the page and click the button again. If the same thing happens, clear your browser’s cache and cookies and start over.

I get a “This site can’t be reached” error.

Ancestry suggests changing the name of your tree to remove any special characters, then trying again. You can find the tree name in Tree Settings.

I have more than one tree.

Each tree exports on its own. Choose the one with the people you want on the chart in step 1.

The download ends in .zip. Is that right?

Yes. Drop the .zip file into Gildroot as it is; it finds the GEDCOM file inside. You can also unzip it first: double-click it on a Mac, or right-click it and choose Extract All on Windows.

My photos are missing.

A GEDCOM file holds names, dates, places and relationships as plain text. Photos stay behind. Charts don’t use photos, so nothing is lost for the chart.
