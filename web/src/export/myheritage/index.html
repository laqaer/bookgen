Export guide

Export your tree from MyHeritage.

MyHeritage exports a tree as a GEDCOM file from its Manage trees page. It takes four clicks and a short wait.

Steps checked against MyHeritage’s own help pages on 30 September 2026. Gildroot is independent and not affiliated with MyHeritage.

Steps, on the MyHeritage website

These are the steps from MyHeritage’s help page for trees kept on the MyHeritage website, on a computer.

  1. Log in to MyHeritage.
  2. Move your mouse over Family tree at the top of the page and click Manage trees.
  3. Find the tree you want. On the right, next to its name, click Export to GEDCOM.
  4. Choose whether to include photos. Gildroot doesn’t use photos, so either choice works for a chart.
  5. Click Begin the export.
  6. MyHeritage prepares the file. If no download appears on the page, look for an email from MyHeritage with a download link, and check the spam folder too.

If you use Family Tree Builder

Family Tree Builder is MyHeritage’s free desktop program. MyHeritage’s help page gives these steps:

  1. Open your family tree in Family Tree Builder.
  2. Click the File menu and choose Export GEDCOM.
  3. Click Save as, give the file a name, and choose where to save it on your computer.

Stuck?

Still stuck after these? Write to support@gildroot.com and say which step. Please don’t attach your file; we never need it.

I can’t find Manage trees.

Manage trees is in the menu under Family tree, at the top of the MyHeritage website. The steps are for the website in a computer’s browser.

No email arrived.

Check the spam or junk folder, and check that the email address on your MyHeritage account is current. If a download link has stopped working, run the export again from Manage trees.

Which tree is mine?

The Manage trees page lists every tree on your family site. Export the one with the people you want on the chart.

The download ends in .zip. Is that right?

Yes. Drop the .zip file into Gildroot as it is; it finds the GEDCOM file inside. You can also unzip it first: double-click it on a Mac, or right-click it and choose Extract All on Windows.

My photos are missing.

A GEDCOM file holds names, dates, places and relationships as plain text. Photos stay behind. Charts don’t use photos, so nothing is lost for the chart.
