Guide

How to print a large family tree.

From a family tree file on your computer to a framed poster on the wall, in seven steps. Most of the time goes into the first two.

By the Gildroot team. Updated 30 September 2026.

1. Decide whose tree, and how far back

A printed family tree is about one person, or one couple. Everything else follows from that choice. For a gift, it is usually the person receiving it, or their parents. For a family wall, choose someone from the youngest generation so that everyone’s ancestors appear.

Then decide how many generations. Each one doubles the number of places:

If your research thins out after a certain generation, stop there or one past it. Gildroot suggests a depth automatically: the deepest generation that is at least a quarter filled. The free ancestor calculator shows how full each generation of your file is.

2. Choose the chart and the size

Three charts suit a wall:

  • A fan chart fits the most names, up to 8 generations.
  • A pedigree chart has room for full places, up to 6 generations.
  • A bowtie chart shows a couple and both their families, up to 6 generations a side.

3. Check the file before you print

A poster makes every typo bigger. Before you order a print, open your file in the free GEDCOM viewer. It lists people with no birth date or birthplace, birthplaces it could not match to a country, and dates that cannot be right, such as a death before a birth. It reads the file on your computer; nothing is uploaded.

If you do not have a file yet, the export guides show how to get one out of Ancestry, MyHeritage, FamilySearch and five other programs.

4. Proof it on paper

Print one Letter-size copy at home at 100%. Read every name aloud with someone who knew the people. Spelling matters more to relatives than any design choice. In the studio you can correct any name, date or place; your original file is never changed.

5. Choose the paper

Matte poster paper suits most charts: no glare, so fine print reads under a lamp and behind glass. Photo paper (luster, satin or glossy) gives deeper darks. The Midnight Gilt style, gold on midnight blue, prints best on photo or matte poster paper and should not go on plain paper.

6. Print it

The same day

Many drugstore photo counters and office print shops print posters within hours. Photo counters usually take a JPEG; print shops usually take a PDF. Call ahead: sizes and papers vary by store.

Online

More papers, and framing if you want it. Allow several business days plus shipping, and more before holidays.

At home

A tiled print splits the poster across Letter or A4 sheets with a quarter-inch overlap, crop marks, and a labeled map on page 1. A 24×36 poster takes 15 Letter sheets. The steps are in the print planner.

7. Frame and hang it

Standard frames come in 11×14, 16×20, 18×24, 20×30 and 24×36, so every Gildroot poster size in inches fits one. A mat around a smaller print makes it look bigger on the wall: an 11×14 print in a 16×20 frame with a mat is a common choice.

Hang it out of direct sunlight, which fades ink over time, and at eye height, so people can find their own names.
