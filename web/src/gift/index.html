Ask the family The answers travel in the link itself

Don’t know every name? Ask.

Start with what you know, then send a link to an aunt or cousin. They fill in the blanks on their own phone and send it back. No accounts, no email to us.

  1. Type in what you know. Names, birth years (or “about 1870”) and where each person was born.
  2. Tap “Ask the family”. Send the link by text or email from your phone, or copy it.
  3. They fill in the gaps. The link opens a short form on their phone: “Help Anna finish the family tree.” They add what they know and tap “Send back”.
  4. Merge it in one tap. Open the link they send you. Anything that differs from what you typed is highlighted, and you choose which to keep.

The names and dates are packed into the part of the link after the # sign, which browsers never send to a website. They pass only through the message you send, to the person you send it to.

Gift questions

I don’t have a family tree file. Can I still make one?

Yes. The Quick Builder is a short form for up to 15 people: the person it’s for, their parents, grandparents and great-grandparents. For each person, fill in whatever you know: name, birth year (or “about 1870”), birthplace and year of death. Blanks are fine. With 4 generations or fewer the chart uses the keepsake layout, with larger type and full dates, so a small tree still looks finished.

Can I make it on my phone?

Yes. The Quick Builder and the Ask-the-family link are made for phones, and you can buy and export on a phone too. Detailed editing of a large tree is easier on a screen at least the size of an iPad.

What if the person I’m giving it to already has a tree on Ancestry?

Then a gift key is usually the better present, because they can make the chart from all of their own research. They export a file from Ancestry and drop it into the studio. How that works.
