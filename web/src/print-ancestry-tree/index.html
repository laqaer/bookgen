Step one: get the file out of Ancestry About two minutes, on a computer

Export your tree as a GEDCOM file

A GEDCOM file is a plain-text copy of your tree: every person, date and place. Ancestry makes one for you. Only the tree’s owner can export it, so if someone shared their tree with you, ask them to send you the file.

These steps follow Ancestry’s own help page, “Uploading and Downloading Trees”, which we checked on 30 September 2026. Menu names change from time to time.

  1. On a computer, sign in to Ancestry. Click Trees at the top of any page and choose your tree.
  2. In the toolbar on the left, click More (the three dots), then Tree Settings.
  3. On the right side of the Tree Settings page, under Manage your tree, click Export tree.
  4. Click Export. The button says “Generating a GEDCOM file” while it works. A large tree can take a few minutes.
  5. When it finishes, click Download Your GEDCOM File. It arrives in your Downloads folder inside a .zip file.

Questions from Ancestry members

Can I export from the Ancestry app on my phone or iPad?

Ancestry’s help page describes the export on its website, so use a computer’s web browser for that one step. After that, you can open the file in Gildroot on the computer, or move it to an iPad and open it there.

Will my photos and stories be on the chart?

No. Ancestry’s GEDCOM file holds the facts of your tree but not photos or other media, and Gildroot charts show names, dates and places. That keeps the chart readable at every size.

What about relatives who are still living?

By default their names show and their dates are hidden. You can change that for the whole chart. Gildroot treats someone as living when there is no death record and they were born less than 100 years ago.

My tree has thousands of people. Will it fit?

A chart shows one person’s ancestors, or one couple’s, not the whole file. You choose who sits at the center, and Gildroot shows as many generations as the size holds: up to 6 on Letter and up to 8 on a 24×36 poster.

Does Gildroot work with DNA matches?

No. The chart is drawn from the family tree file only. DNA results are not part of it.
