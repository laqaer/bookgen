What a fan chart is

A fan chart is a family tree bent into a half circle, or more. The person it is about sits in the center. Their two parents fill the first ring, their four grandparents the second, their eight great-grandparents the third. Every ring holds twice as many places as the ring inside it.

The father’s side fills the left half and the mother’s side the right. So you can see at a glance which lines you have traced a long way back, and which stop early. A place with no name stays as a faint, empty wedge, so a tree with gaps still looks finished.

Gildroot draws fans in three shapes:

  • 180°, a half circle. The default at 5 generations or fewer, printed landscape.
  • 270°, three quarters of a circle, with the title in the open space below the center. The default at 6 to 8 generations.
  • 360°, a full circle.

Who it suits

The family historian
You have traced 5 or more generations and want the whole of it on one sheet. A fan fits 255 names on a 24×36 poster, and shows which lines still have gaps.
A gift for a parent or grandparent
At 4 generations or fewer, Gildroot switches to a keepsake layout: larger type, full dates and places, and an ornamental frame. No file needed: type in up to 15 people.
A family reunion
Center it on someone from the youngest generation and color it by family line, the father’s side and the mother’s side in two tints, so each branch can find its own ancestors.
Someone with a sparse tree
Empty places stay as faint wedges, or you can trim them. It never looks broken.

How to read it

Reading key

Center
The person the chart is about, with the largest name on the page.
Ring 1
Their parents: father on the left, mother on the right.
Ring 2
Their four grandparents. Each sits just outside their own child.
Ring 3+
Great-grandparents, then 2nd great-grandparents, and so on outward.
①
A small numbered circle marks pedigree collapse: one ancestor who fills more than one place, usually because cousins married. The legend says how many times each appears.

Dates print as “24 May 1819” when there is room, and as years (“1819–1901”) further out. “c. 1843” means about 1843.

Questions

Can I choose who sits in the center?

Yes. The studio suggests the person with the most known ancestors, or the home person your family tree program marked. You can search for anyone else in the file and make them the center.

What happens to people who are still living?

By default their names show and their dates are hidden. You can switch to “Living” in place of the name, or show everything. A person counts as living when there is no death record and they were born less than 100 years ago.
