What a bowtie chart is

A bowtie chart is two half-fans facing away from each other, joined by a round medallion. The medallion carries both names, an ampersand, and the couple’s marriage date and place when the file has them.

Each half is a fan of one partner’s ancestors: parents in the first ring, grandparents in the second, and so on outward. Each side is labeled in its top corner with that partner’s family name, or first name when the file has no surname, so a guest can find their own side.

It works for any couple: married or not, same-sex or not. Pick the two people from your file, or build both sides by hand in the studio.

Who it suits

A wedding
A guest-book table, the rehearsal dinner, or a gift from the parents. See bowtie charts for weddings.
An anniversary
A 25th or 50th, with the original wedding date in the center. See anniversary charts.
Your own two sides
Put your parents in the medallion and the chart becomes your own pedigree, split by side.
A couple with no family tree file
Type in what you know in the studio’s Quick Builder, or send an Ask-the-family link so relatives can fill in names from their phones.

How many generations fit

Bowtie numbers are for each side. A 6-generation bowtie holds the couple plus up to 124 ancestors, 62 a side.

The smallest bowtie the studio draws is 3 generations a side.

How to read it

Reading key

Center
The couple: both names, and their marriage date and place if the file records them.
Left
The first partner’s ancestors, labeled with their family name in the top-left corner.
Right
The second partner’s ancestors, labeled in the top-right corner.
Rings
Parents, then grandparents, then great-grandparents, moving outward on each side.
①
A small numbered circle marks pedigree collapse: one ancestor filling more than one place. In the sample family, António Pacheco and Isabel Furtado appear twice.
