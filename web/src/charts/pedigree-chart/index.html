What a pedigree chart is

A pedigree chart is the family tree most people picture from school: one person on the left, and lines branching to the right, doubling at every step. Each person gets a box with their name, birth and death years, and birthplace.

Because the boxes run in straight columns, there is room for full place names, something a fan chart has to shorten. The column headings say who is who in plain words: Parents, Grandparents, Great-grandparents, and so on.

Gildroot draws pedigree charts of 4, 5 or 6 generations, always in landscape. For more generations on one sheet, a fan chart fits up to 8.

How many generations fit

A pedigree needs more room per name than a fan, so it tops out at 6 generations (63 boxes) on the largest posters.

How to read it

Reading key

Left
The person the chart is about.
Upper
At every fork, the upper box is the father and the lower box is the mother.
Columns
Each column is one generation, named at the top.
Boxes
Name, then years (“1870–1938”), then birthplace. “c.” means about; “bef.” and “aft.” mean before and after.
●
A dot marks pedigree collapse, one ancestor who appears in more than one box. The legend below the chart names them.
