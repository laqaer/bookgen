Who we are Independent, not affiliated with any genealogy company

An owner-led, independent studio

Gildroot has one human owner and no investors. We are not part of Ancestry, FamilySearch, MyHeritage or any other genealogy company, and we have no family data to sell: your tree stays on your computer.

The owner decides what matters: prices, the privacy rules, legal pages, partnerships, and anything said in the owner’s name. Gildroot earns its money from chart licenses, paid once, and from the books we publish as Gildroot Press. There are no ads.

How Gildroot is made

In plain words: the software, the guides and much of the day-to-day running of Gildroot are done by AI agents, working under the owner’s direction and review.

That includes writing and testing the code that draws the charts, drafting the help pages and guides, checking facts, and drafting replies to support email. The owner sets the rules they work to, approves anything that involves money, legal terms or the owner’s name, and can be reached at {{site.email.hello}}.

We tell you this because you should know who you are dealing with. Every release of the studio has to pass the same automated checks before it goes live: that every name on a chart prints, that no text is too small to read, that the PDF matches what you saw on screen, and that nothing leaves your computer.

What we never do

These rules apply to the people and the agents alike.

We never invent ancestors

A chart is computed only from your file, or from what you type into the Quick Builder. We never generate people, dates, places or stories, and nothing about your family is written by AI.

We never upload your tree

Your file is read and your chart is drawn inside your own browser. Nothing is uploaded, and an automated test checks that before every release. How that works.

We never fake it

No made-up reviews or testimonials, no countdown timers, no “only 3 left”. When we compare ourselves with others, we give facts and the date we checked them. See the comparisons.

Where our examples come from

Two families appear in our examples. Queen Victoria’s ancestors come from Wikidata, a free public database whose data is released under the CC0 license. We check each person in that chart against Wikidata and Wikipedia.

The Almeida–Novak family is fictional. We made it up as a test file, with the kinds of things real trees contain: accented names, a Greek name, gaps, and a pair of ancestors who appear twice. Wherever it appears, it is labeled as fictional.

The name

Gild, as in gold leaf and heirloom craft. Root, as in roots and ancestry. Our tagline says the same thing in three words: your roots, gilded.

What we care about, in order: charts that are beautiful enough for the wall; charts that are yours, from any file, with every name correct; privacy; being ready tonight; and a fair price, paid once.
